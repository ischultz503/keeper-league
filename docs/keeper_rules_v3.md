# Keeper League Rules — Third Draft

*Updated 8.9.2026 — supersedes Second Draft (8.20.2025). Changes from the second draft are clarifications that fill gaps the old doc didn't address; a changelog is at the bottom.*

---

## 1. Keeper Structure

- Each team may keep up to **3 players** per season.
- Keeper declarations are due **7 days before the draft**.
- Declarations are **final once submitted** and become **visible to the league at the deadline**.
- Once the declaration deadline passes, all forfeited picks are locked in.

## 2. Keeper Costs

**A player's base keeper cost is set on draft day and follows the player all season**, no matter how he is later acquired (trade, waiver claim, drop and re-add):

- **Drafted in Rounds 1–8:** cost = the round he was drafted in. (Drafted in the 5th → keeping him costs a 5th-round pick.)
- **Drafted in Round 9 or later, or undrafted (waiver/free-agent pickup):** cost = a Round 8 pick.

The cost is based on **where the player was drafted, not who drafted him or who keeps him**. A Round 2 player who is dropped and picked up off waivers still costs a Round 2 pick to keep — being added from waivers does not reset a drafted player's cost.

### Escalation for repeat keeps

- The **first keep costs the base cost** (no penalty).
- Each keep after the first costs **one round earlier (more expensive)** than the previous year's cost.
- A player whose cost would escalate past Round 1 cannot be kept again. (This is why a Round 1 pick can never be kept twice.)
- **Maximum keeper length: 3 keeps** — 4 total seasons with the player (draft year + 3 keeps).
- **Keep history and escalated cost follow the player.** If you trade for a player who has already been kept twice, you inherit his escalated cost and his remaining keep count.

### Keeper Cost Progression Chart

| Type | Draft year (Year 1) | Year 2 (1st keep) | Year 3 (2nd keep) | Year 4 (3rd keep) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Round 1 pick | — | Round 1 cost | Cannot keep (escalation) | — |
| Round 2 pick | — | Round 2 cost | Round 1 cost | Cannot keep (escalation) |
| Round 6 pick | — | Round 6 cost | Round 5 cost | Round 4 cost |
| Round 9+ or waiver pickup | — | Round 8 cost | Round 7 cost | Round 6 cost |

## 3. Paying the Cost — Pick Forfeiture Mechanics

- Keeping a player forfeits **a pick you own in the cost round**. Forfeited pick slots are skipped in the draft.
- **If you don't own a pick in the cost round** (e.g., you traded it away), you forfeit your **next-earlier owned pick** instead. *Example: your 4th is gone and you keep a Round-4-cost player → you forfeit your 3rd.*
- **If you own multiple picks in the cost round** (yours plus one acquired by trade), **you choose** which one to forfeit.
- **If two of your keepers have the same cost round**, the first costs that round and the second costs your **next-earlier owned pick**. *Example: two Round-8-cost keepers → you forfeit a Round 8 and a Round 7 pick.*
- If you own no pick in the cost round **or any earlier round**, you cannot keep that player.

## 4. Roster Composition Rules for Keepers

- Only **1 keeper** may have a **current-year keeper cost in Rounds 1–2**. (This is based on what the player costs *this year*, after escalation — a Round 3 draftee on his second keep costs Round 2 and counts against this limit.)
- If keeping **3 players**, at least one keeper must have a current-year cost of **Round 8 or later**.
  - Example of a legal trio: a Round 1 cost + a Round 3 cost + a Round 8 cost.

## 5. Keeper Eligibility

To be kept, a player must meet **at least one** of the following during the season being kept from:

- **Started 4 or more weeks** in his team's lineup (regular season or fantasy playoffs), or
- **Rostered for 9 or more weeks** — time on IR **counts** as rostered.

## 6. Draft Order

- The draft is a **snake draft**. "Your Round N pick" means your draft slot's pick in Round N.
- **Picks 5–10** go to the **6 playoff teams in reverse order of final finish**: league champion picks 10th, runner-up 9th, and so on — the playoff team with the worst final finish picks 5th.
- **Picks 1–4** go to the **4 consolation-bracket teams**, awarded by **consolation finish with choice of slot**: the consolation bracket winner chooses any of picks 1–4 first, second place chooses from what remains, then third place, and fourth place takes the last slot. *(This keeps the best draft position available to non-playoff teams while giving them a reason to win consolation games instead of tanking.)*
- Slot choices happen in order after the championship, on a **48-hour clock per team**; if a team doesn't respond in time, it is assigned the earliest remaining slot and the clock passes on.
- Ties (e.g., platform bracket quirks) are broken by regular-season record, then total points for.

**Transition rule — 2026 draft only.** These rules were adopted after the 2025 season ended, so last season's consolation games were played without anyone knowing they would affect draft position. To avoid rewarding results retroactively, the 2026 draft order is: **picks 1–4 to the four non-playoff teams in reverse order of regular-season standings** (no slot choice), and **picks 5–10 to the six playoff teams in reverse order of final finish**. The consolation slot-choice rule above takes effect with the 2026 season's consolation bracket, first applying to the **2027 draft**.

## 7. Trading Picks and Draft Slots

- Draft picks may be traded, individually or as part of player trades.
- Teams may also **swap entire draft slots** (all rounds) by mutual agreement before the draft.
- Only **next season's picks** may be traded — no trading picks two or more drafts out.
- **Pick trading freezes at the keeper declaration deadline** and reopens after the draft is complete.
- A traded pick is the original team's slot in that round; forfeiture rules (Section 3) apply to whatever picks you own at the declaration deadline.

## 8. Governance

- The commissioner resolves any situation not covered by these rules, with the guidance that rulings should follow the spirit of the closest written rule.
- Rule changes require a league **majority vote** and may only take effect **between seasons** — no mid-season changes.
- Any ruling or rule change is documented in this document with a date.

## Worked Examples (2025 rosters)

**Missing pick (Marcus / JSN).** JSN was drafted in Round 4. Marcus traded his Round 4 pick to Isaac last season. To keep JSN, Marcus forfeits his **next-earlier owned pick — his Round 3**. Isaac keeps the acquired Round 4 pick. (Teams are still free to trade picks around this before the declaration deadline.)

**Composition limit (Isaac).** Ashton Jeanty (Round 2) and Brock Bowers (Round 2) both carry Round 2 costs. Only one keeper may cost Rounds 1–2, so Isaac can keep **only one** of them.

**Same cost round (Isaac).** Rashee Rice (drafted Round 8) and Khalil Shakir (drafted Round 10 → Round 8 cost) both cost Round 8. Keeping both forfeits Isaac's **Round 8 and Round 7** picks.

**Escalation (Isaac).** Ladd McConkey (drafted Round 3): keeping him in 2026 costs Round 3; in 2027, Round 2; in 2028, Round 1 — his final possible keep year (3 keeps max, and cost can't escalate past Round 1).

## Changelog vs. Second Draft (clarifications, not rule changes)

1. Declaration deadline set to **7 days before the draft**; declarations final and revealed at the deadline (was TBD).
2. **Missing-pick rule added**: no pick in the cost round → forfeit next-earlier owned pick (was unaddressed).
3. **Cost follows the player**: base cost is fixed on draft day and unaffected by drops/waivers/trades — closes the drop-and-re-add loophole (old wording could be read as any waiver add costing Round 8).
4. **Same-round collision rule added**: second keeper at the same cost round costs the next-earlier pick (was unaddressed).
5. **Keep history follows the player** across trades (was implied, now explicit).
6. Composition limits defined by **current-year keeper cost** after escalation (was ambiguous "draft cost").
7. Escalation wording clarified: cost moves **one round earlier** per repeat keep (matches the existing chart).
8. Eligibility clarified: playoff weeks count as started weeks; **IR counts as rostered**.
9. **Draft order section added**: reverse final finish for playoff teams (picks 5–10); consolation teams earn choice of picks 1–4 by consolation finish; snake format; slots tradeable. For the 2026 draft only, a transition rule applies (reverse regular-season standings for non-playoff teams) since the 2025 consolation bracket was played before this rule existed.
10. **Trade rules added**: only next season's picks tradeable; pick trading freezes at the declaration deadline.
11. **Governance section added**: commissioner rulings, offseason-only majority-vote rule changes.
